# Product Discovery | From an Everyday Need to a Product

[English](../../README.md) | [日本語](../../README.ja.md)

## The observation

Young children interested in trains often ask adults to read the names of stations on railway maps. They may request the same names repeatedly because the sounds, routes, and sequence of stops are part of the enjoyment.

At the same time, **parents are not always available on demand**. Cooking, household chores, and other responsibilities can make it difficult to read every station name at the moment a child asks.

That created a clear opportunity: **give children a way to hear station names independently, on their own terms.**

## The customer job to be done

> When I want to explore a train line, I want to select a station and hear its name immediately, so I can enjoy the route without waiting for someone to read it aloud.

The app was designed for young train enthusiasts who may not yet be confident readers but can learn through audio, visuals, and simple touch interactions.

## From insight to product requirements

| User need | Product response |
| --- | --- |
| Hear a particular station name | Tap a station for instant audio playback |
| Explore a favorite railway line | Select a line and navigate its stations |
| Enjoy the feeling of a train journey | Next-stop announcements and train movement |
| Use the app with minimal adult assistance | Simple, direct interactions and visual feedback |
| Return to familiar routes | Accessible line selection and recently used lines |

## Feedback from early users

Children who tried the app expressed preferences for particular lines, including:

> “I want to do the Yamanote Line!”

> “I want to hear the line near my home!”

These comments emphasized an important product insight: **relevance and familiarity matter**. Children do not simply want an arbitrary list of station names; they want to choose the routes they recognize and care about.

## Product decisions

**Focus on autonomy:** let the user choose a line and station without navigating a complex menu hierarchy.

**Design for the audience:** prioritize immediate audio feedback and a playful visual representation of train movement.

**Support breadth of interest:** make a wide range of railway lines available rather than centering the experience on only a handful of famous routes.

**Iterate after launch:** refine onboarding, voice controls, and continuous playback to strengthen the overall experience.
